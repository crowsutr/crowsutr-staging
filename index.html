Join us to take on the far right

The majority of people in Britain reject racism and the far right – we can mobilise them. But we need anti-racists organising in every town, city, workplace and community.

Building a movement against Racism

Stand Up to Racism mobilises against racism, fascism, Islamophobia and antisemitism. We do this by bringing together a coalition of anti-racists, trade unions, refugee groups, community groups and more, to build unity against the far right threat.

In practice, this means organising protests, rallies, meetings and more. We believe that it is mass collective action that is decisive.

As well as operating on a national level, we have local groups across Britain who help to coordinate anti-racist and anti-fascist activity in their areas.

Our Story

Stand Up To Racism was formed in 2015 in response to the rise of racism and the far right. We stand in the tradition of mass anti-fascism, following in the footsteps of organisations such as the Anti Nazi League and Unite Against Fascism.

We came together for annual national demonstrations for the United Nations anti-racism day every March. Since then we have organised dozens of national protests against racism, Trump, in support of refugees and in opposition to fascist Tommy Robinson. We consistently hold national conferences and rallies to deepen the anti-racist movement.

At a local level, Stand Up To Racism groups are crucial to building the solidarity necessary for halting the momentum of the far right.

About stand up to racism

Who we are Stand Up to Racism is a broad-based anti-racist organisation that brings together trade unions, community organisations, students, campaigners, faith groups, cultural figures and anti-racist activists.

Our aim is to oppose racism, fascism and the politics of division. We do this by building mass anti-racist and anti-fascist action that unites those who want to oppose the far right’s hate.

What we’re fighting for We are fighting to build a movement against racism, fascism, Islamophobia, and antisemitism.

We want a movement that rejects any scapegoating of refugees, Muslims, Black communities and other oppressed groups. For too long, politicians, the media and the far right have looked to divide ordinary people by blaming these groups for problems instead caused by inequality, austerity and the failures of government.

What we do

We campaign nationally and locally by organising demonstrations, rallies, public meetings, workplace and community events, gigs, conferences, school and college activity and solidarity actions with refugees and migrants.

We believe that unity, solidarity and collective action are the strongest weapons in our arsenal. Our work shows that racists and fascists can be challenged and defeated when we stand together.

How we organise

We are an activist-led organisation. We are coordinated through elected officers and an executive committee, who help organise our national campaigns, events and mobilisations.

Our work is rooted in our local groups that bring together a wide array of people and organisations. It is the activity of these groups that provides the foundation of our campaigns.

We are supported by affiliated trade unions and organisations, whose backing helps strengthen the anti-racist movement in workplaces, communities and campaigns across Britain.

Open Letter: Demand for Accountable Community Safety - Dec 23, 2025

To all community leaders, stakeholders, and statutory agencies:

Stand Up To Racism advocates for collective, systemic, and accountable community safety. We demand the unequivocal rejection of self-appointed vigilante groups in favour of professional, legal, and transparent safety initiatives.

The Risks of Unregulated Patrols

Unregulated patrols are not a legitimate form of protection; they fundamentally undermine community care and the rule of law:

  • Targeting the Vulnerable: These groups often target asylum seekers and refugees, inciting fear and hostility against Black and Asian communities. They threaten social solidarity and provide cover for racist violence.
  • Undermining Justice: Actions such as “private entrapment” or improper evidence collection compromise criminal investigations. Inadmissible evidence leads to collapsed prosecutions and erodes the justice system.
  • Lack of Accountability: These groups bypass mandatory vetting and oversight. Misusing limited “citizen’s arrest” powers (Section 24A, PACE 1984) creates unmanaged risks and potential legal charges for the actors involved, such as false imprisonment.
