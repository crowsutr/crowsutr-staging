Statement – We Stand Together to Protect our Communities

Aug 9, 2026

While the group “Crowborough Says No” claims to represent concerned local residents, the reality is that its platform has been built and supported alongside far-right political figures, extremist elements, and individuals with histories of criminality, violence, and racism.

The marchers flooding our streets are being bused in from all over the country—this is not genuine local concern. Local concern has been hijacked.

They are political agitators who have turned Crowborough into a political circus, disrupting our town and disturbing our peace.

In contrast, Crowborough Stand Up to Racism is truly built by local residents. We are a broad, inclusive coalition of people spanning multiple political affiliations, as well as many with no political alignment at all.

We are united simply by a shared care for our town, our neighbours, and the preservation of our social fabric against a rising tide of hostility and division.

For the past six months, asylum seekers residing at the Crowborough site have faced continuous targeting whenever they leave the grounds. This hostility does not happen in a vacuum. Crowborough is home to around 3,000 long-term residents of diverse ethnic heritage. When asylum seekers are attacked on our streets, our ethnic minority neighbours are made to feel unsafe and targeted in the very town they love and call home.

Furthermore, over the last nine months, local businesses, individuals, and community organisations have faced intense online and in-person harassment simply for showing basic human compassion toward refugees.

This hostility is the direct result of media-driven conspiracy theories, fearmongering, and coordinated campaigns of hatred that have penetrated local communities nationwide.

Standing Against National Hatred & Demanding Local Action

This is not happening in Crowborough alone. Across the country, we are witnessing a dangerous, politically coordinated escalation of anti-immigrant rhetoric and intimidation designed to fracture towns and instigate fear. We look with deep concern and horror at the recent violence in Thetford, where mob rule, violent break-ins, and intimidation forced asylum seekers to be evacuated from their homes for their own safety.

We stand shoulder-to-shoulder with the people of Thetford and every community across the UK facing these attacks. We also call directly on our local council to step up. Our local authority needs better, stronger policies in place to protect our communities from intimidation, safeguard vulnerable residents, and actively celebrate and support the rich ethnic diversity that strengthens Crowborough.

We refuse to let fear, intimidation, and outside agitators define our town. We call on all residents to stand together to show that we will not tolerate hate. Crowborough is a strong, compassionate, and diverse community—and together, we will choose empathy, equality, and unity over division.

Please support the SAMAS appeal and join the silent Crowborough Solidarity Gatherings every Sunday 11am -12noon Crowborough Piazza on Crowborough High Street
