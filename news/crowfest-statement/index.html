Statement on Crowfest

Stand Up To Racism is a very broad based organisation with support from faith and community organisations, trade unions and from across the political spectrum.

It promotes community unity and has been central to the response across Britain to far right violence & attempts to divide our communities. Most recently it led the response to the violent knife attacks on Muslim people in Edinburgh, bringing 5k people onto the streets. It was at the centre of mobilising half a million people in London on the Together Alliance unity demo on 28 March in opposition to growth of the far right.

But SUTR is not a party political organisation and its message, backed by so many nationwide organisations, of unity and solidarity, is surely uncontroversial. It is a trusted ‘go to’ source on antiracism for national media outlets and newspapers.

The cancellation of a Stand Up to Racism stall at Crowborough Town Council’s community event Crowfest, which should be about bringing the community together in unity and celebrating diversity, primarily because of far right pressure and lobbying, is a democratic outrage. One might have thought that anti-racism would have been something every council should support, despite such opposition, as part of its commitment to equality and diversity.

We understand that the strength of the vocal far right minority in Crowborough locally means that many businesses as well as the council have already been forced to abandon activities which might be trying to put ‘unity’ back into the word community. It is deeply worrying and a cause for sorrow and concern that the Town Council’s response to the growth of the far right locally – which has meant intimidation of the local Black and Ethnic Minority community, and the violent intimidation, harassment and ‘doxxing’ of local anti-racist activists in Crowborough – seems to be one of appeasement. The Town Council is apparently happy to allow the far right to utilise council spaces such as Chapel Green to spread their politics of hate and division on a weekly basis, while cancelling an anti-racist stall which aimed to promote a sense of unity and hope at an annual community festival. What kind of message does this send out to the local Black and Ethnic Minority community in Crowborough? Why cant the council proudly champion and defend multiculturalism?

A month or so ago in Crowborough, Crowborough Stand Up to Racism helped support a fantastic Crowborough Unity family friendly event in Crowborough which drew hundreds of people to a peaceful celebration of unity and community.

We urge Crowborough Town Council to reconsider this cancellation decision rather than once again give in to far right political forces who offer only division, violence and hatred.
